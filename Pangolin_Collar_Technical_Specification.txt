SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
================================================================================

1.1 OVERVIEW
The Pangolin Collar is a next-generation wildlife tracking device designed to overcome
the critical limitations of current satellite GPS collars (e.g., Lotek MNR, ATS):
• Excessive weight (900-1200g) limiting species compatibility
• Fixed battery life (1-2 years) requiring recapture or remote drop-off
• High cost ($800-$2000 USD per unit)
• Toxic battery disposal in remote locations
• Limited sensor suite

The Pangolin Collar addresses these through an integrated three-axis energy harvesting
system, advanced materials, and animal-centric design.

1.2 CORE PHILOSOPHY
Design Principle: "Energy-Neutral Autonomy"
The collar operates as an energy-diversified autonomous system, harvesting power from
solar radiation, body movement, and body heat. This eliminates dependence on a single
energy source and enables indefinite operation in optimal conditions.

Species Focus: Medium to large terrestrial mammals
• Moose (Alces alces)
• Gray wolf (Canis lupus)
• Mule deer (Odocoileus hemionus)
• Caribou (Rangifer tarandus)

2.2.2 SCALE ARRANGEMENT & MECHANICAL MOUNTING

Physical Layout:
• 12-16 individual flexible perovskite cells ("scales") laminated onto the collar strap
• Arranged in overlapping, shingled pattern (like reptile scales)
• Spacing: 15mm overlap between adjacent scales
• Coverage: Outer 180° of strap circumference (distributed to maximize light exposure
  regardless of collar orientation)

Mounting Material:
• Thermally conductive adhesive (thermal conductivity > 1 W/m-K)
• Provides both electrical connection and thermal coupling to power management IC
• Substrate: thin aluminum backing (0.3mm) for heat dissipation and structural support

Electrical Topology:
• Scales connected in adaptive series-parallel configuration via high-efficiency diodes
• Parallel subclusters (3-4 scales per cluster) for maximum power point tracking (MPPT)
• Reverse-bias protection: Schottky diodes (1N5818 equivalent) on each scale string
• Voltage regulation: Step-up converter (TI TPS61200 or equivalent) to constant 3.3V
• MPPT Algorithm: Embedded in main MCU, sampling at 100ms intervals

2.3 KINETIC HARVESTING SUBSYSTEM

2.3.1 PENDULUM-BASED KINETIC HARVESTER

Technology: Miniaturized Electromagnetic Induction Harvester
Reference Design: Based on Kinefox LM100 and University of Freiburg prototypes

Principle of Operation:
A small permanent magnet (neodymium NdFeB) suspended on a compliant spring oscillates
within a coil of copper wire as the animal moves. The relative motion between magnet
and coil induces a voltage proportional to acceleration.

2.5.2 ENERGY BUFFER CAPACITY & RESERVE

Total Stored Energy Calculation:
E = 0.5 × C × ΔV²
E = 0.5 × 50F × (10V)² = 2500 J ≈ 695 mWh

Practical Available Energy (80% depth-of-discharge, DoD):
→ Usable capacity: ~560 mWh

This buffer must accommodate:
1. GPS cold-start (acquisition): 40 mWh
2. GPS location fix + processing: 50 mWh
3. LTE-M transmission burst (5-15 seconds): 100-150 mWh
4. LoRaWAN transmission: 15-20 mWh
5. Data processing & logging: 20 mWh
6. Margins & contingency: 200 mWh

Total reserve required per day: 500-600 mWh (easily met by supercap + harvesting)

2.5.3 CHARGING & DISCHARGING CHARACTERISTICS

Charging Curve (from harvesters):
• Charge current limited to 500 mA (protects supercap from stress)
• Charging time (0-80%): ~45 minutes (from full harvesters)
• Charge efficiency: 95%+ (minimal losses in DC-DC converters)

Discharging Curve (during transmission/GPS):
• Discharge current: 100-300 mA (depending on load)
• Discharge time (full to 50% capacity): ~15 minutes at 200mA average
• Temperature effects: Minimal (supercaps stable across -40 to +65°C)

Key Advantage Over Lithium Batteries:
✓ No thermal runaway risk (safe in arctic conditions)
✓ No degradation with shallow cycling (ideal for energy-harvesting duty)
✓ Unlimited charge cycles (no "battery memory")
✓ Tolerates overcharging/overdischarging gracefully
✓ Recyclable and non-toxic

Tertiary MPPT: TI BQ25570 (kinetic harvester)

Key Functions:

• Supercapacitor charging management (current limiting, voltage balancing)
• Load switching (prioritization of CPU, GPS, modem)
• Brown-out detection and system reset logic
• Battery health monitoring (voltage, current, temperature)
• Fuel gauge (remaining energy estimation)

2.6.2 ADAPTIVE DUTY CYCLING ALGORITHM

Logic:
IF supercapacitor voltage > 9.5V THEN
  Enable full operating mode (GPS + LTE-M transmit)
  Take GPS fix every 4 hours
  Transmit data every 6 hours
ELSIF supercapacitor voltage > 8.5V THEN
  Reduce transmission frequency
  GPS fixes every 6 hours
  Transmit data via LoRaWAN (low power) every 8 hours
ELSIF supercapacitor voltage > 7.0V THEN
  GPS disabled, use dead reckoning from accelerometer
  LoRaWAN transmit only (lowest power mode)
  Transmit ping every 12 hours
ELSIF supercapacitor voltage < 7.0V THEN
  Deep sleep (CPU in standby, all transceivers off)
  Wake every 6 hours on timer, take temperature reading only
  Resume normal operation when voltage recovers

Expected Result:
→ Even in worst-case (deep winter, overcast, minimal movement):
  System maintains at least daily location update

3.4.2 MORTALITY DETECTION ALGORITHM

The collar monitors motion and posture to detect potential mortality events:

Algorithm:
IF no significant movement detected for 24+ consecutive hours THEN
  AND body temperature has declined by > 5°C THEN
    AND orientation of accelerometer has not changed THEN
      Flag: POSSIBLE MORTALITY (confidence: high)
      Transmit EMERGENCY alert (immediate priority)
END IF

Fallback Detection:
IF collar has remained stationary for 72+ hours THEN
  Flag: CONFIRMED MORTALITY (confidence: very high)
  Transmit RECOVERY alert
END IF

False Positive Mitigation:
• Hibernating animals: Requires temperature drop + location fixation (prevents winter alerts)
• Sleeping animals: Movement resumption clears flag within 24h
• Stuck collar (mud/entanglement): Manual review of temperature trend

3.5 ONBOARD DATA STORAGE

Non-Volatile Storage: 8GB microSD card (SanDisk Ultra UHS)
File System: FAT32 (maximum 4GB per file, use multiple files)
Data Format: Binary columnar format (not JSON, for space efficiency)

Storage Capacity:
• 500 bytes per location fix (with all sensors)
• 8GB capacity ÷ 500 bytes = 16,000,000 fixes
• At 6 fixes/day = 7,300+ days storage (20+ years autonomy)

Write Strategy:
• Batch writes (minimize power, reduce flash wear)
• Write every 100 fixes or every 2 hours (whichever comes first)
• Secure erase on write-fail (prevents data corruption)

Retrieval Strategy:
• Wireless download via UART over Bluetooth (short-range, low-power debug interface)
• Physical microSD extraction (for maximum data recovery if device fails)

7.3 OVER-THE-AIR (OTA) FIRMWARE UPDATE

Strategy:
• Firmware updates distributed via LoRaWAN or LTE-M (depending on connectivity)
• Maximum update size: 256 kB (compressed, delivered in chunks)
• Secure update: Digital signature verification (ECDSA, 256-bit keys)
• Rollback capability: Keep previous firmware version in flash (safe recovery)

Update Process:
1. Receive firmware image digest over network (integrity check)
2. Verify digital signature (ensure image from authorized source)
3. Download full image in 32kB blocks (minimizes peak power draw)
4. Store in secondary flash partition (no disruption to running firmware)
5. On next boot: Verify image, swap partitions, boot new firmware
6. If new firmware fails to boot, automatically revert to previous version

• Daily surplus: +140 mWh/day

Conclusion: Even in worst-case conditions, device maintains autonomous operation
indefinitely (self-adjusting power budget ensures sustainability).

9.2 PCB DESIGN & LAYOUT

PCB Specifications:
• Layer count: 4 (two signal, two power planes)
• Trace width: 8mil (0.2mm) for power, 6mil for signals
• Via diameter: 12mil (0.3mm) for thermal vias, 10mil for signal vias
• Minimum spacing: 6mil (0.15mm) between traces

Layout Priorities:
1. Power integrity: Star grounding, separate planes for digital/analog/RF
2. RF shielding: Faraday cages around GPS receiver, LTE modem
3. Thermal management: Copper pour around supercaps, high-current traces
4. Signal integrity: Controlled impedance for RF traces (50Ω), short stubs

Component Grouping (thermal management):
• High-power DC-DC converters near supercaps (minimize trace inductance)
• GPS receiver isolated from LTE modem (minimize RF interference)
• Kinetic harvester coil away from RF chains (prevent coupling)

Manufacturing Tolerance:
• PCB thickness: 1.6 ± 0.2mm
• Copper weight: 2oz (70µm) ±20%
• Solder mask: ±0.1mm alignment
• Finish: HASL or ENIG (both acceptable)

9.3 ASSEMBLY PROCESS

Step 1: PCB Fabrication & Component Prep
• Coordinate with fab house for 4-layer design, lead-free solder, RoHS compliance
• Perform incoming inspection (component count, reel verification, datasheet check)
• Bake components at 125°C for 24 hours (moisture removal, standard practice)

Step 2: Surface Mount Assembly
• Paste stencil: 125µm thickness, laser-cut for high-precision
• Reflow profile: Ramp 3°C/sec, peak temperature 260°C, dwell 10-30 sec
• Conveyor speed: 0.5 m/min (allowing proper thermal profiling)
• Solder paste: Sn63Pb37 (for hand rework compatibility) or SAC305 (lead-free)
• Solder quality: IPC-A-610 Class 2 (acceptable for medical/aerospace applications)

Step 3: Manual Component Placement (non-standard parts)
• Supercapacitor stack: Secured with silicone potting, cured 48 hours
• Kinetic harvester assembly: Tested independently before installation
• Thermal interface: Apply 2mm thick thermal paste layer, cure per datasheet

Step 4: PCB Testing & Inspection
• Automated Optical Inspection (AOI): Check for solder bridges, missing pads, misplaced parts
• Continuity test: Verify all power/ground connections
• Dielectric test: Apply 500V DC between power rails (no breakdown)
• Functional test: Apply 3.3V, verify MCU boots to bootloader
• Burn-in: 48-hour operation at full power load (identifies infant mortality)

Step 5: Integration into Collar
• Glue electronics module into polycarbonate housing (silicone RTV, 3M Scotch-Weld)
• Laminate solar scales onto strap (pressure-sensitive adhesive, cure 4 hours)
• Secure thermal fins with anodized rivets (prevents rotation)
• Seal all gaskets with EPDM, tighten fasteners to 2.5 N·m (finger-tight + 1/4 turn)
• Install biodegradable PCL fastener (calibrated for 12-18 month degradation)

Step 6: System Testing & Validation
• Water immersion: 1.5m for 30 minutes (verify IP68)
• Temperature cycling: -40°C to +60°C, 5 cycles (thermal stress test)
• GPS lock test: Acquire fix in <60 seconds (cold start)
• LTE modem registration: Register on network, demonstrate data transmission
• LoRaWAN join: Join network, demonstrate uplink
• Data logging: Record 24-hour continuous operational data
• Documentation: Generate test reports, calibration certificates

14.1 PHASE 2 IMPROVEMENTS (12-24 months post-launch)

Solar Efficiency:
• Upgrade perovskite cells to tandem perovskite-silicon (PCE > 25%)
• Expected yield: +30% daily solar contribution
• Cost impact: +$5-8 per collar

Thermal Optimization:
• Develop species-specific fin geometries (optimize fur penetration)
• Expected yield: +15% thermal contribution
• Research partnership: University of Freiburg (thermal modeling)

Kinetic Harvester:
• Upgrade to tuned multi-frequency resonator (broadens acceleration response)
• Expected yield: +20% kinetic contribution
• Complexity: May require firmware tuning per species

14.2 PHASE 3 ADVANCED FEATURES (24-36 months)

Satellite Communication:
• Add Iridium SBD (Short Burst Data) for far-north deployments
• Trade-off: +50g weight, +$80 cost, but enables >70°N operation
• Expected new markets: Arctic caribou, polar bear research

Machine Learning Activity Classification:
• On-device neural network for gait classification (walk, trot, run, rest)
• Enables behavioral inferences (predator-prey interactions, migration timing)
• Firmware update (no hardware change)

Redundant GNSS Receiver:
• Dual-receiver design for real-time phase differencing (RTK)
• Accuracy improvement: ±2m vs. ±10m (current)
• Use case: Fine-scale habitat selection analysis

14.3 PHASE 4 ECOSYSTEM (36+ months)

Cloud Platform:
• Real-time visualization dashboard (species-specific maps)
• Automated anomaly detection (mortality, extreme temperatures, location outliers)
• Integration with other sensors (camera traps, acoustic recorders)

Collar Upcycling Program:
• Collect used collars post-deployment
• Refurbish (replace supercap, clean housing)
• Redeploy (reduces per-collar cost by 30-40%)
• Circular economy model

Indigenous Community Integration:
• Culturally appropriate technology adoption
• Co-design with local First Nations (respect traditional knowledge)
• Employment of local technicians (collar servicing, data analysis)
