NON-WEAPON DECLARATION (READ FIRST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Noor Enhanced vessel is not a weapon, and this document does not describe
one. The vessel carries no explosive, no warhead, no projectile, no energetic
material of any kind, and no offensive payload of any description. It carries
no capability to damage a vessel, a person, or infrastructure.

The Noor Enhanced is a mine-countermeasure survey and inspection craft. It
performs three civilian and humanitarian functions:

  1. It surveys suspected mined water and maps hazards.
  2. It emits controlled acoustic and magnetic signatures that imitate the
     physical presence of an ordinary ship. This technique, known
     internationally as influence sweeping, encourages a mine to actuate
     against the simulated ship rather than against a real one. The signature
     emitter is a loudspeaker for water and a set of electromagnets. Neither
     component harms anything.
  3. It listens and records, so that clearance teams know where an event
     occurred and which corridor segments stand clear.

The vessel itself remains outside lethal range of any actuation event by
design, and where loss occurs, the vessel is expendable by economics, not by
intent. Every operational claim in this document ties to a stated test in the
verification plan (Section 28), and no claim exceeds what the stated hardware
physically supports.

1. MISSION STATEMENT AND SCOPE BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 MISSION STATEMENT

  The Noor Enhanced provides rapid, low-cost, personnel-safe survey and
  influence sweeping of suspected mined coastal water, with recorded
  verification of every actuation event, so that clearance authorities open
  shipping corridors faster and at a small fraction of traditional cost,
  without exposing any person to the minefield.

1.2 WHAT THE VESSEL DOES

  - Transits autonomously to assigned survey sectors under human supervision.
  - Emits calibrated acoustic and magnetic ship signatures along planned
    sweep lanes (influence sweeping, the same technique family as the
    fielded Mk-104 acoustic generator and magnetic sweep cable).
  - Listens continuously with a hydrophone array and records every impulsive
    event with time, position, and audio.
  - Maps the seabed with side-scan sonar, which sees through the turbid
    and zero-visibility water that blinds optical cameras (Section 15.7).
  - Films the seabed for visual inspection of contacts and post-event
    debris where visibility permits.
  - Reports health, position, and events to a human operator in near real
    time within radio range, and in delayed store-and-forward mode beyond it.
  - Returns for battery exchange, inspection, and reuse. It tolerates loss
    without strategic or environmental harm.

1.3 WHAT THE VESSEL DOES NOT DO

  - It does not classify mines. It cannot determine mine type, age, or fuze
    logic. Classification remains the role of minehunting sonar systems and
    clearance divers.
  - It does not neutralize mines by its own action. An actuation event is the
    mine responding to a simulated ship signature; the vessel adds nothing
    energetic to the water.
  - It does not certify a corridor as safe on its own. It issues clearance
    confidence maps (Section 15). A human clearance authority certifies.
  - It does not operate without human supervision. A trained operator
    supervises every mission, holds abort authority at all times, and reviews
    all verification data before any clearance claim advances.
  - It does not carry any payload outside this specification.

1.4 HUMAN SUPERVISION BOUNDARY

  The operator commands, the vessel executes. Waypoint plans, signature
  activation windows, and abort thresholds come from the operator before
  launch. During the mission the vessel reports; the operator monitors and
  intervenes. The vessel never initiates signature emission outside a
  pre-authorized geofenced window, and it terminates emission on any
  geofence violation, lost-link timeout, or operator abort command.

2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY AND TERMINOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 PHILOSOPHY

  - Low cost through commercial off-the-shelf parts and recycled-material
    structure.
  - Mass production through thermoforming and flat-panel assembly, with no
    specialized shipyard.
  - Personnel safety through full uncrewed operation and expendable economics.
  - Environmental responsibility through electric propulsion, recycled
    polymers, and non-toxic construction.
  - Honesty through testable claims. Every performance figure in this
    document names its verification test in Section 28.

2.2 TERMINOLOGY USED IN THIS DOCUMENT

  Survey craft: the Noor Enhanced vessel.
  Influence sweeping: emission of simulated ship signatures along a lane so
    that influence-fuzed mines actuate harmlessly against the simulation.
  Actuation event: a mine responding to the sweep signature; detected and
    recorded by the verification suite.
  Sweep lane: a planned straight or gently curved track with signature
    emission active.
  Sector: a geofenced survey area, typically 1 km by 1 km.
  Operator: the trained human holding command and abort authority.
  Support boat: the crewed launch, recovery, and supervision platform.

3. REFERENCE OPERATING ENVIRONMENT: STRAIT OF HORMUZ
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The design point is the Strait of Hormuz and its approaches, the most
demanding plausible theatre for this craft. Design values derive from the
figures below; Section 28 tests against each one.

3.1 WATER AND AIR CONDITIONS

  Summer sea temperature: 31 to 34.6 degrees C (July average 32.7 degrees C;
  current readings near 33 degrees C).
  Winter sea temperature: 20 to 23 degrees C.
  Salinity: about 37 to 38 parts per thousand (above the 35 ppt ocean
  average); seawater density 1,026 to 1,030 kg/m3.
  Air temperature: minus 2 to plus 48 degrees C, solar deck loading to
  1,000 W/m2.
  Water clarity: frequently turbid, 1 to 8 m visibility; camera work assumes
  5 m or less and carries its own lighting.

3.2 WIND, WAVES, AND CURRENT

  Shamal wind events (northwesterly, multi-day) produce steep chop of 1 to
  2.5 m significant height in the strait; winter systems exceed this.
  Design operating envelope: Beaufort 0 to 5, significant wave height to
  2.0 m for sweep work and 2.5 m for transit-only, abort at Beaufort 6.
  Tidal streams in the strait reach 2 to 3 kn in narrow sections; the
  navigation budget holds a 3 kn current margin (Section 9).
  Biomarine growth is aggressive in warm, saline water; antifouling and a
  monthly wipe-down cycle are mandatory maintenance items (Section 22).

3.3 THREAT LIBRARY (UNCLASSIFIED, OPEN SOURCE)

  Regional reporting estimates a regional inventory of 2,000 to 6,000 naval
  mines of indigenous, Russian-derived, and Chinese-derived types. Open
  sources name the MAHAM 7 (an influence mine resembling the Italian Manta
  class), the MAHAM 5 influence mine, the Sadaf 01 contact mine, and the
  MAHAM 4 timed limpet device. Bottom influence mines in 10 to 60 m of water
  in the strait approaches form the primary design target; moored contact
  mines form the secondary target. Smart-fuze behaviors (ship counting,
  delay timing, signature discrimination) drive the signature library and
  repeat-pass doctrine in Sections 14 and 19.

3.4 TRAFFIC AND CONSTRAINTS

  The strait carries dense commercial traffic on a defined Traffic
  Separation Scheme. Survey operations coordinate with port authorities and
  broadcast a Notice to Mariners; the craft operates inside declared
  exclusion windows only. Collision avoidance follows COLREGs behavior
  rules (Section 12) with the operator as final authority.

4. REGULATORY AND COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 CLASSIFICATION OF THE CRAFT

  The craft registers as an uncrewed surface survey vessel carrying no cargo,
  no persons, and no restricted payload. It is not a vessel of war, not a
  weapon system, and not ordnance. Signature emitters are acoustic and
  electromagnetic survey instruments in the same family as fisheries sonar
  and geophysical survey equipment.

4.2 APPROVALS PATHWAY (CASE-BY-CASE, HUMAN-SUPERVISED TEST FRAMEWORK)

  Maritime authorities approve uncrewed craft trials case by case, under a
  human-supervised test framework. The Noor program follows this sequence:

  Step 1: Engagement. Brief the national maritime authority, the naval
    mine-countermeasure authority, and the environmental authority on the
    design, the safety case (Section 21), and the test plan (Section 28).
  Step 2: Frequency coordination. License the UHF telemetry band and
    register the satellite and LoRa equipment per national spectrum rules.
  Step 3: Harbor acceptance. Complete dockside and confined-water tests
    under authority observation (Section 28, Phases T1 to T3).
  Step 4: Supervised sea trials. Complete offshore trials with a crewed
    escort, a declared exercise area, and inert mine simulants only.
  Step 5: Operational approval. Obtain an operating letter covering
    defined areas, seasons, crew qualifications, and supervision rules.

  Timeline estimate: 6 to 12 months per jurisdiction, run in parallel with
  prototype construction.

4.3 STANDARDS REFERENCED FOR GOOD PRACTICE

  - COLREGs: navigation and collision avoidance behavior.
  - IEC 60529: ingress protection ratings (Section 16).
  - ISO 12215 (small craft hull construction, scantlings guidance) and
    DNV-ST-0119 or equivalent small-craft load guidance: structural margins.
  - UN Manual of Tests and Criteria, Section 38.3: lithium battery transport.
  - IHO S-57/S-100 chart standards: survey data products.

6. HULL AND STRUCTURAL SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 OVERALL DIMENSIONS

  Length overall (LOA): 5.20 m
  Waterline length (LWL): 4.90 m
  Beam, maximum: 1.60 m
  Waterline beam: 1.35 m
  Hull depth (keel to deck): 0.85 m
  Design draft: 0.22 m (0.30 m maximum with full options and trim ballast)
  Freeboard at full load: 0.55 to 0.63 m
  Full-load displacement: 505 kg (Section 7 closes the mass budget)
  Light-craft displacement: 470 kg
  Hull form: round-bilge monohull, flared bow, transom stern, twin
    propeller tunnels, low profile for reduced windage
  Operational depth: surface craft. The craft does not dive.
  Watertight integrity: sealed hull tested to 10 m equivalent immersion
    (1.0 bar overpressure, external) for 4 hours without measurable
    ingress; this covers capsize and green-water events, not submergence
    operations.

  Correction note: Noor-jo Advanced v2.0 claimed a 100 m depth rating with
  a 5 bar pressure hull. Five bar equals 50 m of water, and a surface-sweep
  craft needs no pressure hull at all. This revision deletes both claims.
  The electronics bays are watertight boxes, not pressure vessels; the
  validation requirement is immersion integrity, not depth rating, and
  Section 28, Test T2 verifies it at 1.5 times the service case.

6.2 HULL MATERIAL: RECYCLED SANDWICH COMPOSITE

  The structure is a sandwich panel: two polymer skins over a recycled
  closed-cell foam core. The sandwich delivers the required stiffness at
  roughly one third the mass of the solid 18 to 22 mm HDPE shell specified
  in the prior revision, and it provides built-in reserve buoyancy.

  Outer skin:
    Material: recycled HDPE sheet (milk-jug and drum regrind, UV
      stabilized with 2% carbon black)
    Thickness: 5 mm standard zones; 8 mm bow impact doubler
    Density: 0.95 g/cm3
    Tensile yield: 25 to 28 MPa; flexural modulus about 1.1 GPa
  Core:
    Material: recycled PET structural foam, closed cell
    Thickness: 20 mm (25 mm in bottom panels)
    Density: 65 kg/m3; compressive strength 0.9 MPa; shear strength
      0.6 MPa
    Function: stiffness, reserve buoyancy (about 0.94 m3 of foam yields
      roughly 900 kg of flotation even with the hull completely flooded),
      thermal and acoustic insulation
  Inner liner:
    Material: recycled polypropylene sheet, 2 mm
    Function: abrasion protection, wiring chase surface, cosmetics
  Reinforcement tape:
    Material: carbon/glass hybrid unidirectional tape, epoxy wet-lay,
      three plies, 100 mm width
    Locations: keel line, bow stem, transom corners, motor pod mounts,
      mast base, lifting points, keel-cooler frames
    Coverage: about 6 m2, 9 kg
  Space frame:
    Material: 6061-T6 aluminum tube, 25 mm OD x 2 mm wall, anodized
    Layout: 4 longitudinal stringers at 300 mm spacing, 9 transverse
      frames at 450 to 600 mm spacing
    Mass: 22 kg
    Function: hardpoint distribution, keel-cooler mounting, mast and
      deck hardware support, lifting load path
  Mass reconciliation: outer skin 21 m2 x 5 mm x 950 kg/m3 = 100 kg;
    inner liner 19 m2 x 2 mm x 905 kg/m3 = 34 kg; core 19 m2 x 0.02 m x
    65 kg/m3 = 25 kg; tape 9 kg; frame 22 kg; bow doubler 14 kg;
    adhesives, weld rod, and sealant 6 kg; primer and topcoat 4 kg;
    deck hardware, hatch, mast, and fittings 12 kg. Hull subtotal 226 kg.

6.3 CONSTRUCTION PROCESS (THERMOFORM, FOAM, BOND)

  Step 1: Thermoform outer skins. Heat 5 mm recycled HDPE sheet to
    200 to 220 degrees C and vacuum-form over a two-piece male mold
    (port and starboard halves plus transom). Cycle time about
    25 minutes per half. Mold: epoxy tooling board over CNC-cut MDF,
    good for 300+ pulls, cost 8,000 to 12,000 CAD amortized across the
    production run.
  Step 2: Thermoform inner liners from 2 mm PP sheet on the same mold
    family with a 20 to 25 mm offset surface.
  Step 3: Foam fill. Place outer skin and inner liner in the assembly
    jig, inject two-part polyurethane/PET-blend foam (or lay pre-cut
    recycled PET foam planks with PU adhesive), cure 12 hours.
  Step 4: Bond hull halves along the centerline with polyurethane
    structural adhesive plus an HDPE weld-rod cap seam. Install the
    aluminum space frame on molded standoffs with adhesive and
    through-bolts at hardpoints only.
  Step 5: Lay carbon/glass tape reinforcement at the listed zones,
    vacuum-bag, cure at 60 degrees C for 6 hours.
  Step 6: Fit the deck ring, hatch coamings, mast base, transom, and
    appendage mounts. Apply epoxy primer (0.2 mm) and two-part
    polyurethane topcoat (0.3 mm) in high-visibility gray with
    retro-reflective tape accents. Apply antifouling below the waterline
    (copper-free, Section 27).
  Build time per hull: 3 shifts of one 8-hour day each for a two-person
    crew once the line runs at rate; 16 to 20 labor hours total.

6.4 STRUCTURAL VERIFICATION (LOADS, MARGINS, FATIGUE)

  Design load cases and margins:

  Case S1, bottom slamming: design local pressure 60 kPa (small-craft
    guidance for this size and speed class in Beaufort 5; verification
    targets the measured peak, not the average). Bottom sandwich panel,
    25 mm core, 300 x 450 mm bay between stiffeners: peak facing stress
    computes to about 11 MPa against a 25 MPa yield, static factor of
    safety 2.3; with the bow doubler the forward third achieves 3.1.
  Case S2, deck green-water: 15 kPa over the foredeck. Deck panel stress
    about 4 MPa; factor of safety above 5.
  Case S3, lifting: two-point davit lift at full load, dynamic factor 2.0.
    Lifting eyebolt load path runs through the aluminum frame; frame
    member stress 38 MPa against 276 MPa yield; factor of safety 7.2.
  Case S4, grounding/dock bump: bow doubler absorbs 5 kN point load;
    core crushes locally by design (sacrificial, repairable, Section 22).
  Case S5, vibration: motor excitation 40 to 75 Hz; first hull panel
    resonance computes above 110 Hz due to sandwich stiffness; isolation
    mounts add 12 dB margin.
  Case S6, dropped craft during handling: 0.5 m drop onto a flat surface;
    corner loads stay inside Case S1 margins with the doubler.

  Fatigue analysis (the prior revision had none):
    Wave encounter cycles per mission: about 25,000 to 60,000 (4 to
      10 hours at 1.5 to 2.5 Hz encounter frequency in Beaufort 3 to 5).
    Service life target: 200 missions, about 8 x 10^6 cycles.
    Recycled HDPE endurance strength at 10^7 cycles: 10 to 12 MPa
      (conservative, knockdown for recycled content and weld seams).
    Maximum cyclic facing stress at Beaufort 5 continuous: about 6 MPa
      rms with 11 MPa peaks. Peaks stay under the endurance strength;
      rms holds a factor of 1.7 to 2.0.
    Weld-seam knockdown: centerline and doubler seams carry a 0.7 joint
      efficiency; seam cyclic stress computes to 4.3 MPa rms, inside the
      knocked-down limit.
    Validation: Test T6 runs a 24-hour continuous wave-tank cycle at
      Beaufort 5 equivalent, then a 200-cycle slam rig at 1.5 times
      design pressure, with pre- and post-test immersion checks and
      dye-penetrant inspection of all seams.

6.5 BUOYANCY SUBDIVISION AND UNSINKABILITY

  The hull divides into three watertight volumes: forepeak (0.4 m3),
  main bay (1.1 m3), and aft machinery bay (0.5 m3), separated by
  foam-bonded bulkheads. The 0.94 m3 of closed-cell foam in the sandwich
  provides about 900 kg of inherent flotation; the craft floats awash but
  upright even with all three compartments fully flooded. No emergency
  blow system exists (a prior-version submarine feature, deleted); the
  foam makes one unnecessary, which removes a compressed-air hazard, a
  failure mode, and 4 kg.

6.6 SHOCK ISOLATION OF ELECTRONICS (NEAR-MISS IMPULSE PROTECTION)

  The standoff protocol (Section 15.3) keeps the hull outside lethal
  blast range, but a near-miss actuation or a heavy slam still delivers
  a water-borne and structure-borne impulse that shatters rigid-mounted
  circuit boards. Military practice isolates electronics on machined
  titanium mounts at roughly 850 CAD per corner; this design achieves
  the same attenuation with commercial stainless wire-rope dampers.

  Hardware: stainless wire-rope isolators, M6 thread, 3 to 15 kg load
    rating per unit, in sets of 4 to 8 per tray.
  Protected items: mission computer tray, hydrophone preamp/ADC tray,
    coil-driver and acoustic-driver plate, side-scan front-end board.
    Battery packs keep their existing neoprene 40A pads (mass rules out
    wire-rope at this size; the steel cases already tolerate 15 g).
  Performance: about 70 percent shock attenuation above 20 Hz;
    operational rating 15 g, survival rating 25 g, 11 ms half-sine.
    The residual impulse at standoff-protocol distances computes well
    inside the 15 g operational rating.
  Validation: Test T21 (20 g shock-machine run with post-test function
    check).
  Mass: 0.6 kg per vessel. Cost: 48 CAD per vessel (Section 24.11).

  carries generous margin for fouling, rain accumulation on deck, and
  future payload growth.

7.2 STABILITY

  Waterplane inertia about centerline: I = 0.7 x LWL x BWL^3 / 12 =
    about 1.19 m4.
  BM = I / displacement volume = 1.19 / 0.499 = 2.38 m.
  KB at 0.22 m draft: about 0.12 m.
  KG from mass budget: 0.35 m.
  GM = KB + BM - KG = 2.15 m.

  This GM is very high; the craft is extremely stiff. A stiff craft rolls
  quickly and transmits harsh accelerations to sensors and structure.
  Roll period estimate with radius of gyration 0.35 x beam:
  T = 2 x pi x 0.56 / sqrt(9.81 x 2.15) = about 0.77 s.

  Design response: the craft accepts high stiffness but manages it.
  Batteries sit on raised trays (0.22 m CG as budgeted, not on the keel)
  to hold GM near 2.0 m rather than higher; the anti-slam bow form and
  1.6 m beam damping from the transom corners absorb the worst of it;
  sensor mounts carry elastomer isolation rated for 8 g. The operator
  speed schedule (Section 19) reduces speed in beam seas above
  Beaufort 4, which caps roll acceleration at the sensor limit. A
  smaller GM is achievable by raising the battery trays further, but the
  anti-capsize margin takes priority in a 2.5 m seaway.

  Heel and trim limits: static heel under 30 kn beam wind computes to
  under 2 degrees; turning heel at 6 kn full differential stays under
  4 degrees; downflooding angle (hatch coaming immersion) exceeds
  50 degrees of heel.

7.3 SEAKEEPING ENVELOPE

  Beaufort 0 to 3 (waves to 1.25 m): full speed schedule, all sensors,
    full signature output.
  Beaufort 4 (1.25 to 2.0 m): transit limited to 5 kn; sweep lanes
    oriented head-seas or following-seas by the mission planner; camera
    use degraded; hydrophone performance unaffected.
  Beaufort 5 (1.8 to 2.5 m): transit only, 3 to 4 kn, no signature
    emission (sweep accuracy degrades below usefulness); the mission
    planner diverts to shelter or holds station.
  Beaufort 6 and above: prohibited. Pre-launch go/no-go and in-mission
    abort both reference this ceiling.
  Pitch and heave at the Beaufort 5 ceiling: pitch amplitude about
  plus/minus 12 degrees, heave about 1.0 m, vertical acceleration at the
  bow about 4 to 6 g, inside the Case S1/S2 structural envelope and the
  8 g sensor mount rating.

10. THERMAL MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (FULL ENGINEERING DESIGN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This section replaces the undefined hull-jacket note in all prior versions.
The review feedback identified the missing engineering: pump selection,
flow-rate calculation, heat-exchanger sizing, worst-case thermal transient,
redundancy, a full circuit schematic, and cost impact. Each item now
follows, with derivations shown.

10.1 HEAT LOAD INVENTORY (WORST CASE: 33 DEGREES C SEAWATER, 45 DEGREES C AIR)

  Continuous sweep-mode loads:
    Motors at sweep power (0.45 kW at 90 percent efficiency): 45 W
    Motor controllers (2 x 2.5 percent of throughput): 22 W
    Acoustic system (220 W average piezo plus 40 W impactor): 110 W
    Magnetic coils, 350 W average, nearly all I2R heat: 330 W
    Coil drivers and acoustic driver electronics: 45 W
    Compute, sensors, comms, side-scan front end (bay heat): 65 W
    Battery internal losses (about 2 percent of draw): 25 W
    DC-DC converters (90 percent efficiency on 150 W): 15 W
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  Total continuous design load: about 660 W (sweep), 950 W (transit with
  signatures on), rounded design figure 1,000 W continuous. Revision 2.1
  note: the side-scan (10 W), dipping hydrophone (negligible), and
  altimeter (3 W) loads fit inside the rounded figures; the downsized
  acoustic amplifier offsets the impactor addition.

  Transient peak (sprint plus full signature output, 30 minutes):
    Propulsion losses 420 W + signature 480 W + electronics 120 W +
    battery 85 W = about 1,105 W; design peak figure 1,500 W with
    margin.

  Note on the review-log arithmetic: the log computed a coolant
  requirement of 28 L/min for 1.2 kW at a 10 degrees C rise. That figure
  contains a units slip. The mass flow for 1,200 W at 10 K is
  1,200 / (4,180 J/kgK x 10 K) = 0.0287 kg/s, about 28 g/s, which is
  1.7 L/min, not 28 L/min. The loop below sizes from the correct
  derivation and still carries generous margin.

10.2 ARCHITECTURE: CLOSED GLYCOL LOOP WITH KEEL COOLERS

  The design rejects the prior passive jacket concept (plastic is a poor
  conductor; 0.45 W/mK HDPE cannot reject 1 kW) in favor of an active
  closed loop:

    Heat sources (cold plates) -> parallel manifolds -> pump pair ->
    two keel-cooler panels (hull-external, flush) -> reservoir ->
    back to cold plates.

  Coolant: 40 percent propylene glycol / 60 percent water (non-toxic,
    freeze margin to minus 12 degrees C, boiling margin above 108
    degrees C), specific heat about 3.9 kJ/kgK, density about 1,040
    kg/m3. Total loop volume 15 L including reservoir.

10.3 FLOW-RATE DERIVATION AND PUMP SELECTION

  Required mass flow at design load 1,000 W and a chosen 4 K coolant rise:
    m_dot = 1,000 W / (3,900 J/kgK x 4 K) = 0.064 kg/s = 3.7 L/min.
  At transient peak 1,500 W the same flow gives a 6 K rise, acceptable.
  Design flow: 6 L/min nominal (1.6 x margin), with pumps selected for
  12 L/min each at the system head so a single pump holds full flow.

  System head estimate: cold plates 8 kPa, panels 10 kPa, plumbing and
    fittings 12 kPa, total about 30 kPa (0.3 bar) at 6 L/min.

  PUMP SPECIFICATION (x2, N+1 redundancy):
    Type: brushless DC centrifugal coolant pump, magnetic drive
      (seal-less, no shaft seal to leak), automotive/marine class
    Rating: 24 VDC (from the 48/24 converter), 45 W input, 12 to 15
      L/min at 0.3 bar, max head 0.9 bar
    Wetted materials: PPS/PA66 housing, ceramic shaft, EPDM static
      seals; glycol-rated
    Control: PWM from the thermal controller; tachometer wire for
      speed feedback (pump-failure detection without a separate flow
      meter in the primary loop; a paddle flow switch provides backup)
    Configuration: parallel installation with flap check valves so one
      failed pump causes zero flow loss; each pump alone delivers
      100 percent of design flow
    Mass: 0.8 kg each; Cost: 130 CAD each
  Pump power draw: 45 W running, 90 W both-pump test mode; the 55 W
    thermal-loop figure in the power budget covers one pump plus
    controller and valve losses.

10.4 HEAT REJECTION: KEEL-COOLER SIZING (LMTD METHOD)

  Rejection hardware: two flush-mounted hard-anodized 6061-T6 aluminum
    keel-cooler panels, each 500 x 800 x 25 mm with ten internal 12 mm
    passages, installed port and starboard at the turn of the bilge,
    total wetted external area 0.80 m2. Panels sit proud of the outer
    skin by 6 mm in a recessed pocket, protected by the skeg line.

  Heat-transfer coefficient budget (design value, conservative):
    External film, seawater at 3 kn: about 1,500 W/m2K
    Internal film, glycol at 6 L/min: about 900 W/m2K
    Wall and anodize conduction: negligible penalty
    Overall U = 1/(1/1500 + 1/900) = 562 W/m2K
    Fouling and aging factor 0.6: design U = 340 W/m2K

  LMTD at the design point:
    Coolant in 48 degrees C, out 44 degrees C; seawater 33 degrees C
    dT1 = 15 K, dT2 = 11 K; LMTD = (15 - 11) / ln(15/11) = 12.9 K
  Capacity: Q = U x A x LMTD = 340 x 0.80 x 12.9 = 3,510 W

  Margin: 3.5 x over the 1,000 W continuous design load at the worst
    summer water temperature. At zero boat speed (loiter, natural
    convection external film about 250 W/m2K, U about 190), capacity
    computes to about 1,960 W, still 1.3 x the full transient peak.
    The loop therefore holds temperatures at anchor, at sweep speed, and
    at sprint, with the reservoir absorbing transients (10.5).

  Component temperature targets at 1,000 W continuous:
    Coolant 44 to 48 degrees C; motor windings under 80 (limit 120);
    ESC cases under 60 (limit 85); electronics bay air under 45
    (component limit 70); battery under 40 (BMS limit 60). All margins
    exceed 20 degrees C.

10.5 WORST-CASE THERMAL TRANSIENT (BOTH PUMPS DEAD AT FULL PEAK)

  Thermal capacitance of coolant: 15 kg x 3.9 kJ/kgK = 58.5 kJ/K.
  Add motor and plate metal about 12 kJ/K; total about 70 kJ/K.
  At the 1,500 W peak with zero active pumping: dT/dt = 1,500 / 70,000
    = 0.021 K/s = 1.3 K/min.
  From a 48 degrees C start, motor windings reach the 60 degrees C first
  alarm in about 9 minutes, the 75 degrees C shed threshold in about
  21 minutes, and the 85 degrees C emergency threshold in about
  28 minutes. Natural convection through the panels (about 1,960 W at
  loiter as computed above) actually arrests the rise below the shed
  threshold in most failure cases; the transient margin exists for the
  sprint case. The crew and the autonomy stack hold more than 20 minutes
  to respond; no single failure creates an urgent thermal event.

10.6 COLD PLATES AND PLUMBING (NAMED HARDWARE)

  - Motor cold plates: 2 x 6061-T6 machined plates, 120 x 90 x 12 mm,
    serpentine 8 mm channel, bolted to motor casing with thermal paste,
    45 CAD each
  - ESC/driver plate: one shared 200 x 150 x 12 mm plate for both ESCs,
    the acoustic driver, and coil drivers, 60 CAD
  - Electronics bay plate: 300 x 200 x 10 mm with integral air-side
    fins and a 40 mm 24 V circulation fan, 55 CAD
  - Manifold: 2 x PPS 4-port manifolds, 22 CAD each
  - Hose: EPDM coolant hose, 12 mm ID, 6 m total, 4 CAD/m
  - Fittings: 16 x brass barb elbows/straights with stainless clamps,

  - Reservoir: 15 L HDPE tank with level sensor port and 0.5 bar

  - Quick-drains: 2 x brass drain valves at loop low points, 8 CAD each

10.7 MONITORING AND FAILSAFE LADDER

  Sensors:
    Motor winding thermistors x2 (one per motor, 6 CAD each)
    Coolant supply and return PT1000 probes x2 (12 CAD each)
    Electronics bay sensor x1 (8 CAD)
    Pump tachometer inputs x2 (built into pumps)
    Paddle flow switch, backup (18 CAD)
    Reservoir level switch (10 CAD)
  Controller: the real-time microcontroller runs this ladder:
    Any motor above 60 degrees C: throttle both motors to 60 percent,
      raise pump to full speed, warn operator.
    Above 70 degrees C: shed the acoustic system (largest discretionary
      load), continue sweep on magnetic only, warn operator.
    Above 75 degrees C: shed magnetic system, transit to mission hold

    Flow switch low or both tachometers zero: treat as above 75 degrees

    Coolant level low: log leak suspect, disable pumps to save them
      from dry running, degrade to convection mode, return home.
  Every threshold logs to the black-box recorder with a 1 Hz thermal
  history for post-mission review.

  FALLBACK: LoRa mesh, 868/915 MHz, 0.3 W, 2 to 5 km per hop, carries

11.2 MESH NETWORK OPERATING MODES (CLARIFYING THE PRIOR OVERCLAIM)

  Radio does not propagate underwater, and this craft operates on the
  surface, so the mesh constraint is simpler than the prior review
  assumed, but the claim of a 50-unit, 250 km self-healing network
  still overstates reality. The honest design:

  Mode M1, surface mesh (normal): up to 12 units per mesh partition
    (tested target; 50-unit networks are a Phase 3 goal, not a
    prototype claim), automatic route discovery, self-healing on unit
    loss, 1 to 5 s packet latency per hop, duty-cycled to stay inside
    band regulations.
  Mode M2, store-and-forward: beyond UHF and mesh range, each unit
    executes its mission autonomously and logs everything; satellite
    SBD forwards a compressed health/event digest every 10 minutes;
    full data uploads on return to link.
  Mode M3, acoustic coordination (option): a commercial underwater
    acoustic modem (about 2,800 CAD, 300 bps, 1 to 3 km) supports
    submerged-to-surface test coordination with instrumented mine
    simulants during trials. It is a trials tool, not a production fit.

11.3 PACKET LOSS AND LATENCY HANDLING

  - Telemetry uses sequence numbers and last-good-value holdover;
    operator display flags stale data after 10 s (UHF) or 3 missed SBD
    cycles.
  - Commands require acknowledgment; unacknowledged commands re-send
    twice, then the autonomy stack applies the lost-link rule
    (Section 12.5).
  - Packet loss above 30 percent for 60 s on UHF automatically promotes
    SBD to primary and logs a link-quality event.
  - All links carry AES-128 encryption and per-unit keys; a failed
    authentication drops the packet silently.

12.3 FAILSAFE STATE MACHINE

  States: IDLE, PRELAUNCH_CHECK, TRANSIT, SWEEP, VERIFY, LOITER,
    RETURN_HOME, ABORT, RECOVERY, SAFE_DRIFT.
  Key transitions:
    Lost link (UHF and SBD both silent 10 min): finish current lane,
      then RETURN_HOME; if battery reserve insufficient, LOITER at the
      last commanded hold point and beacon on SBD.
    GNSS lost over 15 min: dead-reckon to the pre-planned recovery
      corridor, then LOITER and beacon.
    Battery at 12 percent reserve: RETURN_HOME immediately.
    Battery at 5 percent: SAFE_DRIFT, kill signature output, beacon
      every 60 s on SBD and LoRa, strobe light on.
    Single motor fault: continue on one motor, notify, mission
      replans to shorter lanes.
    Both motors fault: SAFE_DRIFT and beacon.
    Water ingress detected (bay flood sensors): isolate affected bay
      power, SAFE_DRIFT, beacon, strobe; foam flotation keeps the
      craft afloat indefinitely.
    Collision detected (IMU impact over 3 g): full stop 60 s, self-
      check, operator alert; resume only on operator confirmation or
      clean self-check after timeout.
    Geofence violation: signature output off instantly, return inside
      fence, alert operator.

12.5 UPDATES AND RECOVERY

  Firmware updates apply dockside over Wi-Fi/Ethernet with signed
    images and A/B partitions; a failed update boots the previous
    image automatically. No over-the-water updates during missions.
  Recovery from software fault: three-level watchdog (process,
    kernel, power-cycle), then real-time controller holds SAFE_DRIFT.

12.6 GNSS-DENIED NAVIGATION (SEABED OPTICAL FLOW PLUS ALTIMETER)

  Threat: GNSS jamming and spoofing are common in contested maritime
  chokepoints. The Section 12.2 inertial solution drifts about 2 percent
  of distance traveled without corrections, which corrupts the survey
  grid over a long lane.

  Military solution and its cost: a Doppler Velocity Log tracks
  ground speed with a multi-beam sonar matrix for about 11,000 CAD
  landed. That price belongs to a different class of program.

  The Noor adaptation: seabed-relative velocity from machine vision.
    - The existing verification camera and LED lights look forward-down
      at the seabed. An optical-flow algorithm (the same physics as the
      sensor inside an optical mouse, scaled to a camera frame)
      measures ground-track velocity directly from bottom texture.
    - A single-beam ultrasonic altimeter (0.3 to 30 m range, 1 percent
      accuracy, 5 Hz, 48 CAD) measures altitude off bottom and gates
      the vision solution: flow data counts only when the bottom sits
      inside the usable 1 to 8 m window.
    - The extended Kalman filter fuses flow velocity, IMU, altimeter,
      and depth-sounder data. Expected drift with bottom in range:
      2 to 5 percent of distance, versus about 2 percent per hour
      growing unbounded for IMU-only dead reckoning.
  Anti-spoofing check: the filter cross-checks every GNSS fix against
    the inertial and mesh-track solution; a position jump over 200 m
    without a matching motion history marks the fix as spoofed and the
    craft ignores it.
  Honest limits: the flow sensor needs the seabed inside camera range
    and some visible bottom texture. Mined littoral chokepoints are
    shallow, so the aid covers the mission area; over deep or
    featureless mud bottom the craft reverts to inertial drift plus
    LoRa mesh relative ranging, and the navigation log marks the
    degraded state. The aid bounds the error; it does not erase it.
  GNSS-out behavior per Section 12.3 remains: dead-reckon to the
    recovery corridor, then loiter and beacon.
  Mass: 0.3 kg. Power: 3 W. Cost: 133 CAD (Section 24.11).
  Validation: Test T20.

14. INFLUENCE SIGNATURE SYSTEMS (SHIP-SIGNATURE SURVEY EMITTERS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These systems imitate the acoustic and magnetic presence of an ordinary
ship. They contain no energetic material. Their purpose is influence
sweeping: presenting a convincing simulated ship so that influence-fuzed
mines actuate against the simulation. The craft performs this work from
standoff distances defined in Section 15.5.

14.1 ACOUSTIC SIGNATURE MODULE

  Primary projector (mid/high band; the Section 14.6 impactor covers
  the 5 to 120 Hz fundamental band):
    Type: cylindrical piezoelectric projector (PZT ceramic), 100 mm
      diameter x 150 mm, shock-isolated mount under the hull centerline
    Frequency range: 120 Hz to 10 kHz
    Acoustic output: up to 300 W peak acoustic; source level about
      185 dB re 1 uPa at 1 m at resonance, verified by Test T11
    Electrical draw: 700 W peak, 220 W average at survey duty
    Drive: compact class-D amplifier from the 48 V bus, 260 CAD
      complete (amplifier, transformer, enclosure); revision 2.1
      downsized this stage because the impactor carries the
      power-hungry low-frequency duty
    Projector cost: 200 to 300 CAD (salvage marine sonar or new
      commercial); budget 240 CAD
  Secondary array: four 50 W broadband transducers (100 Hz to 3 kHz),
    two port and two starboard, giving an omnidirectional hull-noise
    bed under the primary projector, 45 CAD each
  Operating profile: 3 s ramp-in, programmable waveform playback,
    30 s on / 20 s off duty at survey power, 2 s decay; thermal cutout
    at 70 degrees C transducer temperature; cooling from the Section 10
    loop via the shared driver plate plus transducer seawater exposure.

14.2 SIGNATURE LIBRARY (NEW IN THIS REVISION)

  Modern influence fuzes count ship passages, discriminate signatures,
  and delay actuation. A generic tone fails against them. The craft
  therefore plays recorded, ship-class-specific acoustic profiles:

  Library contents (Phase 1 baseline):
    - Handysize bulk carrier, 12 kn: propeller blade-rate fundamental
      about 4 to 6 Hz with harmonics, machinery lines 60 to 400 Hz,
      broadband cavitation to 8 kHz
    - Product tanker, 14 kn: blade rate 5 to 7 Hz variant set
    - Patrol craft, 20 kn: higher blade rate, planing broadband
    - Fishing vessel, 8 kn: low-power diesel signature
  Library format: calibrated FFT templates plus time-series playback
    files; amplitude, blade rate, and harmonic mix randomize inside
    class-realistic bounds on every lane so smart fuzes never see an
    identical repeat.
  Source of recordings: hydrophone recordings of cooperative vessels
    made during Phase 1 trials, plus published open-source ship-noise
    spectra as seed data; every template validates against a tank
    measurement (Test T11) before fielding.
  Coherent multi-physics emission: acoustic playback, magnetic field
    modulation, and (option fitted) pressure pulses emit in a
    coordinated pattern matching a single simulated vessel passing at a
    defined speed, rather than as independent noise sources. The
    Section 14.6 impactor supplies the blade-rate fundamental and
    machinery thump in the 5 to 120 Hz band, strike-synchronized to the
    library file; the piezo projector plays harmonics and broadband
    above 120 Hz. Combined source level matches the revision 2.0
    single-projector 800 W design across the band at roughly two
    thirds of the electrical draw.

14.3 MAGNETIC SIGNATURE SYSTEM

  Configuration: three orthogonal air-core coil pairs (X 1.2 m,
    Y 0.8 m, Z 0.6 m spacing), 80 to 120 turns of 0.75 mm enameled
    copper each, embedded in the hull structure on non-ferrous mounts.
  Field program: DC anomaly 500 to 1,500 nT at 1 m reference, modulated
    1 to 50 Hz, synchronized to the acoustic playback so the magnetic
    and acoustic simulated-ship passages coincide.
  Power: 300 to 500 W continuous per dominant axis at full output;
    40 percent duty at survey profile gives the 350 W average carried
    in the power budget; coil heat flows into the Section 10 loop
    through driver plates and coil-form conduction paths.
  Drivers: three MOSFET H-bridge boards with current feedback, 95 CAD
    each; wire and forms 90 CAD; winding labor in-house 6 h.
  Calibration: three-axis magnetometer survey at 5, 10, and 25 m in a
    magnetically quiet site after every coil service (Test T12).

14.4 PRESSURE-INFLUENCE MODULE (OPTION, NON-EXPLOSIVE)

  Pressure-fuzed mines respond to the hydrodynamic pressure field of a
  moving hull, the hardest influence to simulate. Two honest options:

  Option P1 (baseline): hull-form pressure field. The 5.2 m hull at
    3.5 kn generates a modest but real pressure signature; combined
    with repeated passes and the coherent acoustic/magnetic program it
    covers older pressure fuzes partially. Cost: zero. Effectiveness:
    partial, stated honestly.
  Option P2 (optional sparker): a 100 J capacitor-discharge underwater
    spark source (400 V bank, tungsten electrodes in a sealed,
    double-insulated chamber), one pulse per 15 s, adding a broadband
    pressure/acoustic impulse. This is a survey sparker in the same
    family as geophysical seismic-survey equipment; it contains no
    explosive and produces no fragment. Power 200 W average when
    active. Cost: bank 150 CAD, charge circuit 85 CAD, electrode
    chamber 65 CAD, integration 55 CAD, total 355 CAD. Fitted only
    where the threat assessment calls for it.

  Neither option claims full pressure-mine coverage. Deep-water
  pressure-influence sweeping at full ship scale remains beyond this
  craft class (even national programs treat it as unsolved for small
  USVs); Section 30 lists it as a standing limitation.

14.5 SIGNATURE SYSTEM COST SUMMARY (CAD)

14.6 LOW-FREQUENCY MECHANICAL IMPACTOR (REVISION 2.1)

  Mission need: acoustic-influence mines actuate on the low-frequency
  hull and machinery vibration of a passing ship, mostly the 5 to
  120 Hz band. Producing high-energy sound in that band with
  piezoceramic projectors demands large amplifiers and heavy battery
  draw. Basic mechanical impact physics converts a simple electrical
  pulse directly into a low-frequency acoustic shockwave in the water
  at a fraction of the cost and power. (Impact-type emitters have a
  long pedigree in influence sweeping for exactly this reason.)

  HARDWARE:
    Actuator: heavy-duty 24 VDC solenoid, automotive starter class,
      about 60 N strike force, tungsten-carbide striker tip
    Strike target: tuned 3 mm spring-steel plate, 350 x 350 mm,
      bonded to the inner hull skin on a spring suspension; plate
      resonance about 40 Hz; the hull skin radiates the impact into
      the water
    Driver: MOSFET low-side board with flyback protection and current
      sense, 35 CAD
    Mounting: frame hardpoint beside the keel line; striker travel
      12 mm; elastomer snubber limits plate excursion

  OPERATING PROFILE:
    Strike energy: about 35 J electrical per strike, 150 W peak
    Strike rate: programmable 0.5 to 10 Hz, synchronized to the
      signature-library blade rate of the simulated ship
    Duty: about 15 percent at survey profile; average draw 40 W
    Acoustic output: source level 150 to 160 dB re 1 uPa at 1 m in the
      10 to 120 Hz band, verified by Test T19, comparable to the
      low-frequency output of the revision 2.0 eight-hundred-watt
      piezo design
    Thermal: solenoid coil temperature monitored; 50 percent maximum
      duty enforced in firmware; coil heat (about 35 W average at
      survey duty) conducts to the Section 10 loop through the shared
      driver plate; a coil overtemp triggers the standard thermal
      ladder

  ROLE IN THE SIGNATURE PLAN: the impactor carries the low-frequency
  fundamental; the downsized piezo projector (Section 14.1) carries
  harmonics and broadband. Neither replaces the magnetic or optional
  pressure influences.

  Mass: 3.5 kg. Cost: 265 CAD (solenoid 55, plate and suspension 45,
  driver 35, integration labor 2 h at 65 CAD/h 130).

15. EVENT DETECTION AND VERIFICATION SUITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The verification suite answers the question that defines the mission:
did anything actuate, where, and with what confidence. This section
adds the standoff protocol and the multi-factor confidence model that
prior versions lacked.

15.1 HYDROPHONE ARRAY

  Transducers: three omnidirectional hydrophones in a 0.8 m triangular
    cluster under the hull, titanium/acetal housings, 40 CAD each
  Sensitivity: minus 190 dB re 1 V/uPa; flat 10 Hz to 100 kHz
  Front end: low-noise preamp (60 dB, 0.5 nV/rtHz), 24-bit ADC at
    96 kHz (upgraded from the prior 48 kHz to capture blast rise
    times), 110 CAD
  Event capture: 10 s pre/post ring buffer per event; blast impulses
    from typical bottom mines measure 160 to 200+ dB re 1 uPa at 1 km,
    comfortably inside dynamic range; detection range 1 to 5 km by mine
    size, validated by Test T13
  Localization: time-difference-of-arrival across the cluster gives
    bearing; cross-fixing between two or more craft, or successive
    fixes from one moving craft, yields position; single-craft fix
    error about 50 m at 1 km, dual-craft under 20 m
  Classification: the prior version claimed a neural network trained on
    naval archives, an unavailable dataset. This revision trains the
    impulse classifier on published impulsive-source spectra plus the
    program's own instrumented simulant recordings, and it presents
    classifier output as one confidence factor among several (15.4),
    never as sole evidence.

15.2 CAMERA AND LIGHTS

  Camera: 1080p30 low-light module, 90 degree wide lens, 5 lux minimum,
    in a 30 m rated polycarbonate dome housing, forward-down looking,
    45 CAD module plus 120 CAD housing
  Lights: two 1,000-lumen LED modules on the camera axis (turbid
    Hormuz water needs them), 30 CAD each
  Recording: 128 GB industrial microSD onboard, continuous during
  VERIFY passes; one compressed frame per 5 s streams over UHF
  Pan-tilt option: 2-axis servo mount, 140 CAD

15.3 STANDOFF PROTOCOL (NEW IN THIS REVISION)

  Blast physics first: at 10 m from a 50 kg-class mine actuation,
  peak shock overpressure measures in the hundreds of psi and fragments
  travel at 50 to 200 m/s; the craft's 1 bar immersion rating is
  irrelevant against that. The craft therefore never closes a live
  contact. The protocol:

  Rule 1: Signature emission and sweeping proceed on lanes whose
    offsets keep the craft at least 100 m laterally from any
    suspected contact location (acoustic and magnetic influences
    propagate to the mine; the craft stays away).
  Rule 2: On an actuation detection, the craft logs time and fix,
    holds position, and waits 60 s for shock dissipation and
    secondary effects.
  Rule 3: Verification approach stops at 50 m for hydrophone and
    camera debris-field inspection; closer approach to 20 m occurs
    only when no actuation followed a sweep pass and the camera
    inspects the contact, and only on operator command.
  Rule 4: Any contact that fails to actuate after two sweep passes is
    flagged UNCONFIRMED for the clearance authority; the craft does
    not escalate by closing in. Escalation is a human decision with
    proper disposal assets.
  Rule 5: Swarm lanes de-conflict so no craft sits inside 300 m of
    another craft's active sweep cell during an emission window.

15.4 MULTI-FACTOR EVENT CONFIDENCE (REPLACES SINGLE-THRESHOLD LOGIC)

  Background acoustic environment in Gulf waters: harbor and traffic
  noise 120 to 140 dB, whale vocalizations to 190 dB, sonar pings,
  seismic activity. A bare dB threshold generates false clears. Every
  candidate event therefore scores:

    Confidence = f(impulse shape and spectral match,
                     magnetic anomaly correlation in time,
                     visual debris-field confirmation,
                     coincidence with own signature emission window,
                     position inside the swept lane cell,
                     cross-confirmation by a second craft)

  Reporting grades:
    CLEARED-EVENT: impulse during own emission window, spectral match,
      and either second-craft confirmation or visual debris within
      30 minutes. Two independent factors minimum.
    PROBABLE-EVENT: impulse plus one supporting factor.
    UNCONFIRMED: anything else, including silence after sweeping.
  A corridor segment earns a clearance-confidence percentage, never a
  bare cleared stamp; the human clearance authority owns final
  certification.

15.5 POST-SWEEP VALIDATION MODE (NEW IN THIS REVISION)

  After the sweep phase, surviving craft run the corridor a second
  time with all signature systems OFF:
    - Hydrophones listen for residual acoustic anomalies.
    - The side-scan sonar (Section 15.7) strip-maps every validation
      lane regardless of turbidity, flagging mine-like objects.
    - The camera surveys the seabed lane for undetonated ordnance and
      debris fields where visibility permits.
    - The depth sounder re-checks for newly exposed proud objects.
  Anomalies flag for manual verification. Post-mission analysis
  correlates all event fixes with any known minefield intelligence,
  computes per-100 m segment clearance confidence, flags low-
  confidence segments for follow-up, and generates a clearance
  certificate draft with GPS coordinates for the human authority.

15.6 VERIFICATION SUITE COST SUMMARY (CAD)

  Hydrophones 120; preamp/ADC 110; camera 45; housing 120; lights 60;
  storage 25; mounts and connectorization 85; materials 565 CAD;
  integration labor (6 h) 390 CAD. Subtotal: 955 CAD. Pan-tilt option:

15.7 SIDE-SCAN SEABED SONAR (ZERO-VISIBILITY VERIFICATION)

  Mission need: in the turbid coastal water of the operating theatre,
  optical cameras go blind below 1 to 2 m of visibility and sediment
  plumes blind them entirely. Acoustic side-scan imaging illuminates
  the seafloor with sound instead of light, producing strip-maps that
  reveal mine-like objects (MLOs) and seabed hazards regardless of
  water clarity.

  Military solution and its cost: dedicated survey side-scan systems
  with proprietary processing run 3,800 to 6,000 CAD and up. The
  piezoceramic element inside a consumer fishfinder transducer emits
  the same high-frequency pulses; the cost lives in the proprietary
  software and enclosures, not in the physics.

  THE NOOR ADAPTATION:
    Transducer: consumer-marine dual-frequency side-scan element
      (455/800 kHz class, 3-in-1 type), port and starboard fan beams,
      mounted in a streamlined fairing under the hull centerline,
      tilted 20 degrees, 280 CAD
    Front end: custom analog board (low-noise preamp, band-pass
      filters, 24-bit ADC) feeding raw ping data to the mission
      computer, 85 CAD in parts; the board design is the one piece of
      genuine engineering development in this subsystem
    Processing: onboard software builds waterfall strip-maps in real
      time; a contrast-based MLO cueing detector flags objects 0.3 m
      and larger; thumbnails of every cue stream to the operator over
      UHF; full strip-maps record onboard and attach to the clearance
      certificate draft
    Fallback: if the custom front end misses Test T18 performance
      targets, a complete consumer side-scan head unit with network
      logging output bolts on instead for about 250 CAD additional,
      with zero hull changes

  PERFORMANCE TARGETS (TEST T18 VERIFIES):
    Range: 90 m per side at 455 kHz; 45 m per side at 800 kHz
    Detection: 0.3 m MLO at 40 m slant range, 800 kHz, in visibility
      under 1 m (where the camera sees nothing)
    Swath productivity: a validation pass at 4.5 kn maps a 90 m swath,
      about 0.75 km2 per hour

  Mass: 1.1 kg. Power: 10 W. Cost: 615 CAD (transducer 280, front-end
  board 85, fairing mount 55, integration labor 3 h 195).

15.8 GRAVITY-DROP DIPPING HYDROPHONE (REVISION 2.1)

  Mission need: the hull-mounted hydrophone array sits in the surface
  noise layer. In Beaufort 4 to 5, wave turbulence and hull slap raise
  the local noise floor and mask the blast signatures the verification

  Military solution and its cost: a motorized dipping winch with a

  THE NOOR ADAPTATION (GRAVITY OVER SERVOS):
    Element: one spare-class omnidirectional hydrophone, minus 190 dB

    Line: 12 m Kevlar-reinforced signal cable with a 0.6 kg

    Release: electromagnetic pin release (24 V, 5 W momentary); the
      weight drops the element to depth in under 5 seconds, 25 CAD
    Operation: the line streams behind the craft during loiter and
      validation passes; the element feeds the same preamp/ADC bank as

    Recovery: manual wind-in at recovery, or sacrificial cut-away in

  Mass: 1.4 kg. Power: negligible (5 W momentary at release).
  Cost: 185 CAD (element 40, line and reel 55, release 25,

17. LAUNCH AND RECOVERY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Launch and recovery decides the real-world tempo of uncrewed MCM
operations; this section treats it as a first-class design problem.

17.1 LAUNCH METHODS

  Method L1, davit or crane (primary): two-point lift at the frame
    hardpoints (SWL 400 kg each, Case S3), from the support boat or a
    shore crane. One person rigs, one operates the davit. Time to
    water: 4 minutes.
  Method L2, ramp/beach: the craft slides off a inflatable roller
    ramp; two persons, 10 minutes; suitable for expeditionary sites.
  Method L3, trailer at boat ramp: standard small-boat trailer,
    1,400 CAD (fleet asset); 15 minutes.
  Pre-launch sequence (automated PRELAUNCH_CHECK): seal vacuum-decay
    spot check, battery state above 90 percent, link check on all three
    radios, GNSS fix, failsafe self-test, geofence upload, operator
    go/no-go signature. Total 12 minutes.

17.2 RECOVERY METHODS

  Method R1, davit: the craft holds station against the support boat;
    crew attaches a snap-hook to the bow recovery eye (the craft
    presents the eye via a floating pickup line), then lifts. Sea
    state limit Beaufort 4.
  Method R2, net/ramp recovery: for training waters, the craft drives
    onto a submerged ramp net. Sea state limit Beaufort 3.
  Method R3, self-beaching (emergency): on SAFE_DRIFT near shore the
    craft grounds on the bow doubler, which is sacrificial by design;
    recover with two persons and the roller ramp.
  Recovery in rough weather: above Beaufort 4 the craft loiters in a
  pre-planned lee holding area until conditions ease; the battery
  reserve always includes 2 hours of holding power for this case.

17.3 TRANSPORT

  One craft fits a standard pickup bed or a 6 m flatbed (3 across).
  Tie-down at the frame hardpoints; mast collapsed; battery packs
  travel installed (UN 38.3 certified, state of charge under 60
  percent for road transport per carrier rules).

18. OPERATIONAL CONCEPT (CONOPS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

18.1 ORGANIZATION

  A standard detachment: 5 craft, 1 support boat (7 to 9 m), 1 shore
  station, 15 battery packs (10 installed, 5 rotating), 4 personnel
  (mission lead/operator, second operator, two technicians).

18.2 MISSION CYCLE

  Planning: the mission lead imports known or suspected mine
    coordinates (minefield mapping integration), charts, traffic, and
    weather into the planning tool; the tool generates geofenced
    sectors, parallel sweep lanes at 50 m spacing with 100 m contact
    offsets (Section 15.3), lane assignments per craft, and a battery/
    swap schedule. Planning time: 1 to 2 hours per 10 km2.
  Deployment: craft launch per Section 17, transit in Trail formation
    at 6 kn under UHF supervision.
  Execution: craft run assigned lanes with signature emission inside
    authorized windows only; the operator monitors position, health,
    and event detections on the shore display; every actuation event
    logs automatically with a confidence grade.
  Intervention: the operator holds abort, hold, reroute, and
    return-home authority on every link. Lost-link behavior per
    Section 12.3.
  Verification: post-sweep validation passes (Section 15.5), then
    recovery, data upload, and the post-mission analysis package.
  Mission termination: signature emission stops on geofence exit,
    abort command, battery reserve, or thermal ladder action. Recovery
    per Section 17.2.

18.3 SWARM COORDINATION

  Five craft partition a sector by lanes; the LoRa mesh shares
  position and event fixes every 5 s; lane de-confliction enforces
  the 300 m emission-window separation (Section 15.3, Rule 5); one
  craft's UNCONFIRMED contact automatically queues a validation pass
  for the nearest other craft.

18.4 RULES OF CONDUCT

  - The craft never emits signatures outside an authorized geofence
    window.
  - The craft never approaches a contact closer than the standoff
    protocol permits.
  - The craft yields to all crewed traffic (Section 12.2).
  - The operator logs every intervention; the log attaches to the
    clearance certificate draft.

19.3 REFERENCE MISSION: 10 KM CORRIDOR, 15 SUSPECTED MINES

  Assets: 5 craft, 4 personnel, 1 support boat.
  Day 1: survey and validation mapping, 2 craft, weather window.
  Days 2 to 4: doctrine sweeping, 5 craft, two sorties each per day.
  Day 5: post-sweep validation and low-confidence re-sweep.
  Output: event log with graded confidence per contact, clearance
    certificate draft with per-segment confidence, flagged
    UNCONFIRMED contacts for disposal assets.
  Expected time per actuated mine: 15 to 30 minutes of lane time,
  versus 1 to 2 days per contact for crewed sweeping, a 95 percent
  timeline reduction on the volume-clearing task.

21. SAFETY CASE AND HAZARD ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

21.1 TOP-LEVEL SAFETY CLAIMS

  Claim A: no person enters the minefield; all craft operations occur
    uncrewed with the crew on the support boat outside the swept area.
  Claim B: the craft carries nothing energetic; a lost craft is a
    floating plastic and electronics object, recovered or sunk-in-
    place benign (Section 27).
  Claim C: every failure mode terminates in a defined safe state
    (Section 12.3); no failure mode creates a hazard to third parties
    beyond ordinary small-craft drift, mitigated by the beacon,
    strobe, AIS broadcast of position, and Notice to Mariners.

21.2 HAZARD LOG (SEVERITY x LIKELIHOOD, MITIGATION, RESIDUAL)

  H1, actuation blast damages craft: severity high, likelihood medium.
    Mitigation: standoff protocol (100 m minimum), expendable
    economics, foam flotation. Residual: accepted; craft loss costs
    under 20,000 CAD and zero personnel risk.
  H2, collision with commercial traffic: severity high, likelihood
    low. Mitigation: AIS watch, COLREGs behavior, operator authority,
    high-visibility paint, strobe, exclusion windows, Notice to
    Mariners. Residual: low.
  H3, loss of control link: severity medium, likelihood medium.
    Mitigation: triple-link architecture, lost-link state machine,
    geofence. Residual: low.
  H4, flooding: severity medium, likelihood low. Mitigation: three
    watertight compartments, sealed sub-enclosures, flood sensors,
    900 kg foam flotation (craft cannot sink). Residual: low.
  H5, propulsion failure: severity low, likelihood medium.
    Mitigation: dual motors, limp-home, SAFE_DRIFT, recovery plan.
    Residual: low.
  H6, battery thermal event: severity high, likelihood very low.
    Mitigation: LiFePO4 chemistry (thermal runaway threshold far
    above operating range), dual BMS, 60 degrees C hard cut, steel
    cases, bay flood tolerance, thermal loop. Residual: very low.
  H7, thermal overload of signature systems: severity low, likelihood
    medium (hot Gulf water). Mitigation: Section 10 system plus
    ladder; Tests T8 to T10. Residual: low.
  H8, magnetic/acoustic emission affects third parties: severity low,
    likelihood low. Mitigation: geofenced windows, marine-mammal
    observation protocol (Section 27), emission power limits.
    Residual: low.
  H9, recovery injury (lifting): severity medium, likelihood medium.
    Mitigation: davit procedure, SWL-rated gear, PPE, training.
    Residual: low.
  H10, capsize in Beaufort 5+: severity low, likelihood low.
    Mitigation: GM 2.15 m, downflooding angle above 50 degrees,
    self-draining deck, immersion-rated seals, upright-floating foam
    trim. Residual: very low.

21.3 ABORT LOGIC AND EMERGENCY STATES

  Abort triggers (any of): operator command, geofence violation,
  battery reserve boundary, both-pump thermal event past the ladder,
  flood in two bays, sustained link loss with GNSS degradation.
  Abort action: signature off, RETURN_HOME or LOITER-hold by battery
  math, beacon on, operator notification on every link.
  Emergency states and recovery procedures live in the state machine
  table (Section 12.3) and drill in the simulator course (Section 20).

22. MAINTENANCE AND SERVICING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

22.1 SCHEDULE

  After every mission (1 h per craft): freshwater rinse including
    cooling panel faces, visual hull check, propeller and shaft check,
    hatch vacuum-decay spot check, data download, battery recharge and
    log review.
  Every 25 operating hours (2 h): shaft-seal inspection and grease,
    keel-cooler face inspection and soft-brush cleaning, connector
    inspection, hydrophone dome wipe, camera dome polish, flood-sensor
    function test, pump tachometer comparison.
  Every 100 hours or monthly (4 h): full seal grease, coolant level
    and refractometer check (40 percent glycol verified), pump bench
    flow check, magnetic calibration of IMU, hydrophone pistonphone
    check, structural tap-test of sandwich panels around hardpoints,
    antifouling condition check (warm Gulf water demands it).
  Every 200 hours or annually (8 h): replace all O-rings and the
    shaft lip seals, replace coolant, bench-test ESCs and coil
    drivers, propeller pitch check and balance, full immersion
    re-test (Test T2 procedure), battery capacity test per pack,
    update firmware, review fatigue log against Section 6.4.
  Corrosion control: the only seawater-exposed metals are 316 shafts,
    Ni-Al-bronze propellers, anodized aluminum panels and frame ends;
    zinc anodes (2 x 0.5 kg) on the transom protect them; inspect
    quarterly, replace at 50 percent consumption.

22.2 BATTERY CARE

  Charge to 100 percent only before missions; store at 50 to 60
  percent; never leave packs below 10 percent; capacity-test every
  6 months; retire packs at 80 percent of nameplate; recycle through
  a licensed lithium recycler (Section 27).

22.3 STORAGE PREPARATION

  Over 30 days idle: rinse and dry, remove batteries to storage
  charge, open hatch vents, desiccant packs in each bay, cover from
  sun, monthly systems wake-up self-test.

26. COMPARISON WITH FIELDED MINE-COUNTERMEASURE SYSTEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Grounding in real programs (sources in Section 32.6):

  US Navy MCM USV (Textron CUSV class): diesel, aluminum hull,
    semi-autonomous, tows the Mk-104 acoustic generator and magnetic
    sweep cable for influence sweeping, or the AQS-20C minehunting
    sonar. Payload delivery systems alone: 18.3 M USD for four
    minehunt PDS units (Raytheon, 2025 contract) and 12.1 M USD for
    minesweep PDS (Textron, 2025 contract). Craft plus payload plus
    support runs to millions per unit; mission packages cost tens of
    millions.
  Textron MMUSV (2026): 13,000 lb payload, Sea State 5, a larger and
    far more capable, far more expensive class of craft.
  Exail Inspector 90/125: 9.2 m aluminum and 12.3 m GRP craft, 24 to
    40 h endurance, 20 kn, multi-million-euro class systems with
    towed sonar and disposal ROV options.
  Barracuda expendable neutralizer: an underwater munition with a
    warhead; a different mission (destruction at the mine) and a
    weapon by design. Noor Enhanced is not in this category and does
    not compete with it; Noor sweeps and surveys, it never destroys.

  Where Noor Enhanced fits: it is not a replacement for these
  systems. It performs volume influence sweeping and verified
  actuation mapping at 19,800 CAD per craft, ahead of and alongside
  the exquisite systems, so that:
    - crewed and high-end uncrewed assets spend their hours on
      classification and disposal, not on sweeping;
    - suspected areas convert into graded confidence maps in days;
    - attrition of a 19,800 CAD craft is a budget line, not a
      program loss.

  Mission cost comparison, 10 km corridor with about 15 suspected
  contacts (Section 19.3 scenario):
    Crewed minesweeper deployment (90-day class operation):
      16 M USD class (about 22.6 M CAD), personnel inside the threat
      area.
    Commercial high-end USV package: 5 M USD class (about 7 M CAD).
    Noor Enhanced detachment (5 craft + month of operations +
      spares + 2 attrition replacements): 5 x 19,804 + 27,000 +
      6,000 + 39,600 = about 171,600 CAD.
  Cost ratio: better than 100 to 1 against crewed sweeping for the
  volume-clearing task, with zero personnel exposure. Prior versions
  claimed 95 to 1 on optimistic pricing; honest pricing improves the
  ratio further because traditional costs never fell.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
27. ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

27.1 MATERIALS FOOTPRINT

  Each craft diverts about 120 kg of post-consumer HDPE/PP/PET from
  the waste stream. Propulsion is fully electric: zero fuel, zero
  exhaust, zero bilge discharge (sealed hull, no water intake beyond
  the closed-loop coolers, no oil aboard other than 200 ml of
  gear-case lubricant). Copper-free antifouling avoids biocide
  leaching.

27.2 ACOUSTIC EMISSION MANAGEMENT

  Signature emission is loud by design, so the program manages it:
    - Emission occurs only inside authorized windows and geofences.
    - Duty cycle caps at 60 percent; total emission hours per sector
      are counted and limited.
    - A marine-mammal watch (visual plus the craft's own hydrophones,
      which detect vocalizations to 190 dB) precedes every emission
      window; detection of cetacean presence inside 1 km delays
      emission 30 minutes.
    - Ramp-in at 3 s gives mobile fauna time to depart.
    - Seasonal exclusion applies near documented breeding or
      aggregation areas per the environmental authority.

27.3 LOSS AND END OF LIFE

  A lost craft floats (foam) and presents a navigation hazard no
  worse than driftwood; the beacon and last-known position drive
  recovery. Unrecovered hull material is non-toxic, non-leaching
  polymer; batteries in sealed steel cases remain recoverable for
  years. End of life: hull regrind returns to the sheet supplier,
  electronics and batteries go to licensed recyclers, target 85
  percent material recovery by mass.

28. VERIFICATION AND VALIDATION PLAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every performance claim in this document maps to a test below. Nothing
advances to operational use on the strength of analysis alone.

28.1 FACTORY AND DOCK TESTS

  T1 Factory acceptance: full power-up, all sensors read, all links
    pass, thermal loop pressure test at 1.5 bar, 30-minute full-power
    motor run on the bench rig, vacuum-decay on all enclosures.
  T2 Immersion integrity: sealed hull submerged to 10 m equivalent
    (pressure tank or weighted pool test), 4 hours, zero measurable
    ingress; repeated after every 200-hour service.
  T3 Electromagnetic checkout: coil drivers at full output beside the
    mission computer, GNSS, and hydrophone front end; no interference
    above noise floor permitted.

28.2 SHALLOW-WATER TESTS (PROTECTED HARBOR)

  T4 Baseline maneuvering: speed-power curve measured (validates
    Section 8.3), turning circles, limp-home on one motor, trim fin
    authority.
  T5 Stability: measured GM by inclining test (validates Section 7.2
    within 15 percent), downflooding check, capsize-recovery drill
    with crane.
  T6 Structural: 24-hour continuous Beaufort 5 equivalent in a wave
    basin plus 200-cycle slam rig at 1.5 x design pressure; dye-
    penetrant inspection of seams before and after (validates
    Section 6.4 fatigue case).

28.3 SYSTEM TESTS

  T7 Endurance: full-charge mission profile run until reserve
    boundary; measured endurance validates Section 9.2 within 10
    percent.
  T8 Thermal soak: 4-hour full-signature run in 33 degrees C water;
    pass = no load-shed event, bay air under 45 degrees C.
  T9 Single-pump failure: inject at full load; pass = zero alarms,
    flow within 5 percent.
  T10 Both-pump failure: pass = ladder executes; first alarm no
    earlier than 8 minutes; emergency threshold no earlier than 25
    minutes.
  T11 Acoustic calibration: source level and library fidelity measured
    against reference hydrophone at 1, 10, and 100 m in representative
    water; ship-profile templates approved for fielding only after
    this test.
  T12 Magnetic calibration: three-axis field mapped at 5, 10, 25 m;
    repeat after any coil service.
  T13 Verification suite: instrumented inert simulant trials measure
    detection range, fix error, and classifier false-alarm rate
    against traffic noise, marine life recordings, and sonar
    interference; pass = detection of the reference impulse at 1 km,
    fix error under 50 m single-craft, false-alarm rate under 1 per
    24 operating hours.
  T14 Comms: UHF walk-away range test, SBD message-cycle timing, LoRa
    mesh partition healing after unit removal, lost-link drill
    (validate Section 12.3 transitions).

  T18 Side-scan imaging: runs over instrumented MLO simulants (0.3 m
    and 0.5 m) in stirred-sediment or natural turbid water with
    visibility under 1 m; pass = detection and strip-map localization
    of every simulant at 40 m slant range at 800 kHz, plus a
    comparison run documenting that the optical camera detects none
    of them in the same water.
  T19 Impactor output: reference hydrophone measurement of source
    level and spectrum in the 5 to 120 Hz band at 1 and 10 m; pass =
    150 dB re 1 uPa at 1 m minimum at the blade-rate lines, plus a
    4-hour duty run with no coil overtemp event on the thermal
    ladder.
  T20 GNSS-denied navigation: GNSS-disabled lane run over shallow
    bottom (under 10 m); pass = lane-keeping error under 5 percent of
    distance over a 2 km lane; a deep-water run documents the
    inertial-only degradation for the record.
  T21 Shock isolation: shock-machine run, 20 g, 11 ms half-sine, on
    each wire-rope isolated tray; pass = full function post-test with
    no connector, solder, or mount failure; complements the T6
    structural program.

30. LIMITATIONS AND HONEST ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The craft does not classify mines and does not replace minehunting
    sonar or disposal systems. It is a volume-clearing and
    verification tool ahead of those assets.
  - Pressure-influence sweeping at full ship scale remains partial
    (Section 14.4); deep, modern pressure fuzes do not always respond.
  - Smart fuzes with ship counting and long delays demand repeat
    passes and patience; clearance rates in Section 19 already carry
    that penalty.
  - Verification confidence is probabilistic (Section 15.4); a human
    clearance authority certifies corridors, the craft informs.
  - Beaufort 6 stops operations; Gulf shamal events pause the
    calendar.
  - Satellite latency (20 to 60 s SBD cycle) forbids remote joystick
    piloting beyond UHF range; the craft is a supervised autonomous
    system, not a remote-controlled boat.
  - Every performance figure awaits its Section 28 test; this
    document commits to test-derived revision, not marketing figures.
  - Side-scan imagery detects and localizes mine-like objects; it
    does not classify them, and its resolution sits below military
    synthetic-aperture systems. The same honesty rule applies: the
    craft informs, the authority certifies.
  - The optical-flow navigation aid needs the seabed inside camera
    range (about 8 m) and some bottom texture; over deep or
    featureless bottom the craft reverts to inertial drift. The aid
    bounds navigation error; it does not eliminate it.
  - The mechanical impactor covers the low-frequency band only;
    full-band signatures still need the piezo projector.
  - The dipping hydrophone line is sacrificial by design; high-tempo
    operations consume drop-lines as a planned consumable.
  - An actuation event near the craft (inside protocol distances due
    to an unknown contact) risks craft loss; the economics absorb it,
    the foam keeps the debris afloat, and no person is exposed.

32.3 GLOSSARY

    influence-fuzed mines actuate harmlessly against the simulation.
  Actuation event: a mine responding to the sweep signature.

  Keel cooler: hull-external flush heat-exchanger panel.
  N+1: one redundant unit beyond the required set.
